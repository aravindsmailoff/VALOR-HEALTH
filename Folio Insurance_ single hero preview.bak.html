Every clear Friday

The dome opens at dusk and closes when the last person leaves, which in December is about nine and in June is about two in the morning. There is a kettle and there are no chairs.

  1. Fri 22 Aug

    Saturn at opposition

    Rings open to twenty-six degrees, the best they will be this decade. The 8-inch refractor will be on it all night.

    21:10
  2. Fri 29 Aug

    Double stars for beginners

    Albireo, Mizar, and the one everybody argues about. Bring nothing; we will show you how to find them again from your garden.

    21:00
  3. Fri 05 Sep

    The Perseid tail

    Past the peak and therefore worth watching, because there is nobody else on the hill.

    20:45
  4. Fri 12 Sep

    Lecture: parallax

    How the first person to measure the distance to a star did it with a brass instrument and eleven months of patience.

    19:30

What is under the dome

  • I

    The Cooke

    An 8-inch Cooke refractor of 1888, on its original clock drive. It is the reason the society exists and it is still the best instrument on the hill.

    Aperture
    203 mm
    Focal
    2 900 mm
    Built
    1888
  • II

    The Grubb

    A 5-inch Grubb from 1902, mounted on the south pier. Easier to point, easier to share, and the one every visitor actually looks through.

    Aperture
    127 mm
    Focal
    1 520 mm
    Built
    1902
  • III

    The transit

    A meridian transit instrument that has not been used in earnest since 1961 and is kept working out of stubbornness.

    Aperture
    76 mm
    Focal
    910 mm
    Built
    1874
